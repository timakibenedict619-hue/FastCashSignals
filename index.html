What is Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency created in 2009 by Satoshi Nakamoto. Unlike traditional currencies controlled by governments, Bitcoin operates on a peer-to-peer network without central authority.

With a fixed supply of 21 million coins, Bitcoin is often called "digital gold" due to its scarcity and store-of-value properties. Every transaction is recorded on the blockchain—a public, immutable ledger.

Fixed Supply

Only 21 million BTC will ever exist. 19.6M already mined. Scarcity drives value.

Decentralized

No bank or government controls Bitcoin. Network runs on thousands of nodes worldwide.

How Bitcoin Works

Understanding the technology behind the world's first cryptocurrency in 4 simple steps.

1

Transaction

Alice wants to send 1 BTC to Bob. She creates a transaction using her private key.

2

Broadcast

Transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin network and picked up by miners for verification.

3

Mining

Miners solve complex math problems to add the transaction to a block on the blockchain.

4

Confirmation

After 6 confirmations (~1 hour), Bob receives the BTC. Transaction is irreversible.
